Governance Quality Index

A comparative index exploring governance through rule of law, freedom, control of corruption, and government effectiveness.

About the GQI

What the index measures

The Governance Quality Index (GQI) is an index created by GeographikaStatistics to compare the quality of governance across countries and territories. It combines four established measures, each representing a different dimension of governance.

World Justice Project

Rule of Law

Measures the quality of justice and the extent to which the rule of law is upheld.

Freedom House

Freedom

Measures freedom, including political freedom, which is particularly important to the GQI.

World Bank WGI

Control of Corruption

Assesses perceptions of the extent to which public power is used for private gain, including both petty and grand forms of corruption.

World Bank WGI

Government Effectiveness

Reflects public-service quality, civil-service quality, policy formulation and implementation, and the credibility of government commitments.

Methodology

How the scores are combined

1. Normalize each component

Each source score is normalized to a scale from 0 to 1. A ranking factor is then added to account for the country's or territory's position relative to the other entries in that source index.

Ranking factor = (N + 1 − r) / N

N is the number of countries and territories ranked in the source index. r is the entry's rank in that index.

2. Calculate the overall score

The four adjusted component scores are combined using their arithmetic mean to produce the overall GQI score.

GQI = (Rule of Law + Freedom + Control of Corruption + Government Effectiveness) / 4

The overall score therefore reflects all four dimensions rather than relying on only one measure of governance.

How to read the scores: The component values shown in the ranking table are the adjusted scores, not the original 0–1 normalized values. Because the ranking factor is added, adjusted component scores can approach 2.

Limitations and scoring philosophy

I generally prefer indexes in which 1 represents the average, values below 1 indicate below-average performance, and values above 1 indicate above-average performance. However, not every indicator has a meaningful average, and different indicators can have very different distributions. For example, one indicator's maximum might be five times its average, while another's might be only 1.8 times its average. Combining them without accounting for those differences can distort the results.

The current methodology is an attempt to keep scores more concentrated around 1 while treating 2 as a general upper reference point. It does not perfectly achieve the original goal, and the methodology remains a work in progress. The GQI should be understood as a comparative index, not a complete description of every aspect of governance in a country or territory.
